    How a 10-Minute Kitchen-Table Session Works

    Open the box and place the [FACT] comparator lid on the table. Try these three hands-on challenges with your child in 10 minutes:

    STEP 01 • MINUTE 1–3
    Prove That Order Doesn't Matter (\(4 \times 9 = 6 \times 6\))

    Have your child build 4 × 9 in the left socket (two blue [2]s + two red [3]s) and 6 × 6 in the right socket ([2]+[3]+[2]+[3]). Both towers reach the exact same 36.2 mm height—proving both factor trees equal 36.

    STEP 02 • MINUTE 4–7
    Solve GCF & LCM Without Formulas

    Build 12 ([2]+[2]+[3]) on the left and 18 ([2]+[3]+[3]) on the right. The blocks they share in common ([2] and [3]) give GCF = 6. Snap one missing block onto the shorter tower until both stand flush at LCM = 36.

    STEP 03 • MINUTE 8–10
    3-Second Stepped-Floor Cleanup

    Stack the blue [2]s, red [3]s, white [5]s, and tan [7]s back into their 12 stepped box columns. When all 43 blocks are in place, every column stands completely flat at 42.0 mm.

    CLASSROOM INVESTIGATIONS

    • Prime vs. Composite: Primes are single indivisible blocks; composites are multi-block stacks.
    • Commutative Property: \([3] + [5] = [5] + [3] = 15\) (stacking order does not change height).
    • Greatest Common Divisor: Find \(\gcd(18, 24)\) by comparing overlapping blocks \([2] \times [3] = 6\).
    • Least Common Multiple: Extend stacks until heights equalize; \(\text{lcm}(4, 6) = 12\).
    • Logarithmic Multiplication: Concrete precursor to Napierian logarithms, where adding block heights executes multiplication.
