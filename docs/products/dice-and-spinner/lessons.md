  <!-- ==========================================================================
       LESSON 1: THE LAW OF LARGE NUMBERS
       ========================================================================== -->
  <section class="ec-section">
    <div class="p-6 bg-slate-900/60 border border-slate-700/60 rounded-xl">
      <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2">
        <span class="ec-pill ec-pill-category">Lesson 01</span>
        <span class="text-xs text-gray-400">Target Grades: 6&ndash;8 &bull; Standard: CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.7.SP.C.6</span>
      </div>
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-2">The Law of Large Numbers: 10-Sector Frequency Convergence</h2>
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-300 mb-6">
        <strong>Lesson Objective:</strong> Students conduct 10-spin, 50-spin, and combined classroom 200-spin trials to observe how relative frequency converges toward theoretical probability \(P = 0.10\).
      </p>

      <!-- Concept Illustration Card -->
      <div class="mb-6 rounded-lg overflow-hidden border border-slate-700/80 bg-slate-950/60 p-4">
        <img src="../../../assets/renders/dice-spinner-craftsmanship.png" alt="Precision 18mm Die Pips and Low-Friction Spinner Dial" class="rounded w-full mb-3" />
        <div class="text-xs text-gray-300 flex items-center justify-between">
          <span><strong>Empirical Precision Instruments:</strong> 18 mm precision chamfered d6 die with centered pips paired with a low-friction balanced pointer needle.</span>
          <span class="text-amber-400 font-mono">Fair Randomization</span>
        </div>
      </div>

      <h3 class="text-base font-bold text-sky-400 mb-3">Step-by-Step Classroom Investigation</h3>
      <div class="space-y-4 mb-6">
        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">1.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Determine Theoretical Probability:</strong> Inspect the 10-sector uniform dial (sectors labeled 0 through 9). Each sector spans an identical \(36^\circ\) angle. Calculate:
            \[
            P(\text{Sector } 7) = \frac{1}{10} = 0.10 = 10\%
            \]
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">2.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Run a Small Sample Trial (N = 10):</strong> Spin the needle 10 times. Record tallies on a frequency table. Calculate the experimental frequency: e.g., if 7 appeared 3 times, relative frequency is \(\frac{3}{10} = 30\%\). Note the expected high variance.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">3.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Expand to Medium Sample (N = 50):</strong> Spin 40 more times (total 50). Recalculate relative frequencies. Notice how extreme spikes begin to flatten.
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">4.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Combine Classroom Data (N = 250+):</strong> Pool data across 5 lab groups. Plot a bar graph on the board. The bars will form a remarkably uniform distribution, proving that large sample sizes smooth out short-term noise.
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- Misconception Diagnostic Box -->
      <div class="p-4 bg-amber-950/40 border border-amber-600/40 rounded-lg mb-6">
        <h4 class="text-sm font-bold text-amber-300 mb-1">&Delta; Key Misconception Diagnostic: "The Gambler's Fallacy"</h4>
        <p class="text-xs text-amber-100/90 leading-relaxed mb-2">
          <strong>The Common Error:</strong> If the needle lands on Sector 3 three times in a row, students claim "Sector 3 is hot" or conversely "Sector 3 cannot land again because it's due for a different number."
        </p>
        <p class="text-xs text-amber-100/90 leading-relaxed m-0">
          <strong>The Physical Fix:</strong> The spinner has no mechanical memory. The aerodynamic needle starts every spin independently. Prove this by showing that \(P(\text{Sector } 3)\) on spin #4 remains exactly \(\frac{1}{10}\).
        </p>
      </div>

      <!-- Independent Transfer Task -->
      <div class="p-4 bg-slate-800/80 border border-slate-600/60 rounded-lg">
        <h4 class="text-sm font-bold text-emerald-400 mb-1">&check; Independent Transfer Check</h4>
        <p class="text-xs text-gray-200 leading-relaxed m-0">
          <strong>Prompt:</strong> If a student spins the 10-sector dial 500 times, approximately how many times would you expect it to land on an even number \(\{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\}\)? Explain your reasoning.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <!-- ==========================================================================
       LESSON 2: COMPOUND PROBABILITY & SAMPLE SPACES
       ========================================================================== -->
  <section class="ec-section">
    <div class="p-6 bg-slate-900/60 border border-slate-700/60 rounded-xl">
      <div class="flex items-center gap-2 mb-2">
        <span class="ec-pill ec-pill-category">Lesson 02</span>
        <span class="text-xs text-gray-400">Target Grades: 7&ndash;10 &bull; Standard: CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.7.SP.C.8</span>
      </div>
      <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-2">Compound Events: Die Roll + Spinner Dial Sample Spaces</h2>
      <p class="text-sm text-gray-300 mb-6">
        <strong>Lesson Objective:</strong> Model two-stage independent compound probability events by rolling a mass-balanced die alongside spinning a sector dial, constructing tree diagrams and coordinate grids.
      </p>

      <h3 class="text-base font-bold text-sky-400 mb-3">Step-by-Step Classroom Investigation</h3>
      <div class="space-y-4 mb-6">
        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">1.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Enumerate the Compound Sample Space:</strong>
            <ul class="list-disc pl-5 mt-1 text-xs text-gray-300 space-y-1">
              <li>Die outcomes: \(\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}\) (6 outcomes)</li>
              <li>Spinner 4-Color outcomes: \(\{\text{Red}, \text{Blue}, \text{Green}, \text{Yellow}\}\) (4 outcomes)</li>
              <li>Total sample space size: \(6 \times 4 = 24\text{ equally likely compound outcomes}\).</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">2.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Calculate Compound Probability:</strong> Find \(P(\text{Roll } > 4\text{ AND Spin Red})\):
            \[
            P(\text{Roll } \in \{5, 6\}) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}, \quad P(\text{Red}) = \frac{1}{4}
            \]
            \[
            P(\text{Compound}) = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12} \approx 8.33\%
            \]
          </div>
        </div>

        <div class="flex gap-3 text-sm text-gray-200">
          <span class="font-bold text-sky-400 min-w-6">3.</span>
          <div>
            <strong>Conduct Empirical Verification:</strong> Perform 36 trials, rolling the die and flicking the spinner simultaneously. Count occurrences of \((5, \text{Red})\) and \((6, \text{Red})\) to verify experimental alignment with the theoretical \(\frac{1}{12}\).
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- Misconception Diagnostic Box -->
      <div class="p-4 bg-amber-950/40 border border-amber-600/40 rounded-lg mb-6">
        <h4 class="text-sm font-bold text-amber-300 mb-1">&Delta; Key Misconception Diagnostic: "Adding Instead of Multiplying"</h4>
        <p class="text-xs text-amber-100/90 leading-relaxed mb-2">
          <strong>The Common Error:</strong> Adding independent event probabilities: \(\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{12}\).
        </p>
        <p class="text-xs text-amber-100/90 leading-relaxed m-0">
          <strong>The Physical Fix:</strong> Draw a \(6 \times 4\) grid on paper. Each square represents one outcome. Shading only the matching squares shows clearly that only 2 out of 24 boxes qualify, proving that compound probability multiplies.
        </p>
      </div>

      <!-- Independent Transfer Task -->
      <div class="p-4 bg-slate-800/80 border border-slate-600/60 rounded-lg">
        <h4 class="text-sm font-bold text-emerald-400 mb-1">&check; Independent Transfer Check</h4>
        <p class="text-xs text-gray-200 leading-relaxed m-0">
          <strong>Prompt:</strong> Draw a tree diagram showing all possible outcomes of rolling a fair die and spinning a 10-sector dial. How many total outcomes exist? What is the probability of rolling an odd number and spinning a prime number?
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
