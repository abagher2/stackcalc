## Teaching & Instrument Architecture Notes

### Inside-Out Sight-Reading & Syntax Anxiety Elimination
In competitive mathematics and timed STEM examinations, errors frequently stem from algebraic input friction rather than conceptual misunderstanding. Complex nested structures—such as radical pyramids ($\sqrt{17 + \sqrt{29 + \sqrt{83}}}$) or multi-tier continued fractions—force algebraic users to enter deep parentheses and defer evaluation until the final keystroke. When an unmatched parenthesis or syntax fault occurs, problem pacing is destroyed. Reverse Polish Notation resolves this by structuring calculations from the inside out or from the bottom up. In continued fractions, climbing from the lowest denominator via reciprocal `{{key:1/x}}` yields verified sub-totals on the transflective LCD at every step. This immediacy provides real-time verification and eliminates syntax anxiety under strict time constraints.

### Tactile Feedback & Peripheral Verification in Rapid Problem-Solving
Solving dense problem sets (such as 70 multi-topic geometry, physics, and calculus problems in 30 minutes) requires seamless coordination between document sight-reading and key actuation. The SC-32's 400g snap-dome tactile switches provide decisive acoustic and tactile feedback, enabling practitioners to enter values by touch while keeping visual focus fixed on problem statements. The continuous 4-level stack display allows peripheral verification of intermediate values without interrupting input cadence.

### Keystroke Economy & Register Preserving Architecture
In rapid computational environments where seconds determine outcomes, the SC-32 eliminates over 350 parenthesis keystrokes across a 70-problem set compared to infix calculators. Elevating harmonic combinations to a dedicated hardware key (`{{key:x∥y}}`), incorporating single-keystroke polar/rectangular transformations (`{{key:▸θ,r}}`, `{{key:▸y,x}}`), and preserving the four-register stack contract ($X, Y, Z, T$) with automatic register drop on binary operations convert multi-step algebraic procedures into compact, deterministic keystroke pipelines.
