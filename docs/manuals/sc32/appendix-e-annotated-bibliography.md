# Appendix E: Primary Sources & Annotated Further Reading Guide

The architecture of the StackCalc SC-32 does not exist in isolation. It represents the conscious convergence of more than a century of research across mathematical logic, stack-oriented computer organization, numerical error analysis, human-factors engineering, and scientific instrument craftsmanship.

While the historical narratives of William Hewlett's 1972 Palo Alto pocket calculator challenge, John Napier's 1614 logarithms, Cody and Waite's range-reduction polynomials, Eratosthenes' geodesy, Carl Friedrich Gauss's 1801 orbital recovery of Ceres, and Claude Shannon's 1937 Boolean switching algebra have been woven directly into the chapters of this book, this appendix provides an annotated guide to the primary historical, mathematical, and engineering literature cited throughout the volume.
