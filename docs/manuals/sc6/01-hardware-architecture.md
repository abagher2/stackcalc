## Meet Your Stack Calculator: The Screen, The Number Stack & Your Secret Shift Keys

Before we build block towers and solve puzzles, let's take a quick tour of your calculator: its daylight screen, the 4-level number stack, the big ENTER button, and the secret shift keys.

### The Daylight Screen & The 4-Level Number Stack ($T, Z, Y, X$)
Unlike phones or tablets that shine bright electric light straight into your eyes, the SC-6 features a **Daylight Screen**. It reads just like paper in a book, catching the natural light in your room!

On the screen, numbers don't vanish into hidden memory. Instead, they stack up on four visible shelves—the **4-Level Number Stack** ($T, Z, Y, X$), just like stacking physical blocks, pancakes, or books on your desk:
- **Level $X$ (The Ground Shelf / Active Entry):** This is the bottom shelf of the screen where numbers appear as you type them, and where the immediate results of every calculation appear.
- **Level $Y$ (The Second Shelf / Waiting Partner):** When you tap `{{key:ENTER}}`, your number pushes smoothly up to Level $Y$. Level $Y$ holds the first number waiting for math to happen (like the $12$ waiting in $12 - 4$).
- **Levels $Z$ and $T$ (The Mezzanine and Top Shelves):** Above $Y$ sit two more shelves. When you push several numbers onto the stack, previous values rise into $Z$ and $T$. When operations execute, values drop down from $T$ to $Z$ to $Y$ automatically, letting you solve big multi-step problems without scratch paper or parentheses!

### The Big ENTER Button: Pushing Numbers Onto the Stack
Look at the big, friendly black `{{key:ENTER}}` button in the lower-right area of the keypad. It is the heart of **Stack Math**!

In ordinary school arithmetic, you might have been taught to fear the equals sign (`=`) because it acts like a sudden test. On the SC-6:
1. **There is no `=` button:** In Stack Math, combining two numbers *is* the answer! When you tap `{{key:+}}`, `{{key:-}}`, `{{key:×}}`, or `{{key:÷}}`, the operator fuses the quantities on Level $Y$ and Level $X$ immediately and visibly.
2. **`{{key:ENTER}}` commits and pushes:** When typing a number, tapping `{{key:ENTER}}` signals that entry is complete, pushes the value of Level $X$ upward onto Level $Y$, and lifts previous values into $Z$ and $T$. The next digits you type enter fresh into Level $X$ without overwriting Level $Y$.

### One Button, Three Superpowers: How the {{key:f}} and {{key:g}} Keys Work
Instead of hiding cool math tools inside confusing computer menus, each button on the SC-6 has up to **three superpowers**:
- **Primary White Keycap (Direct Press):** Tapping any key directly executes its primary action printed in crisp white on the keycap face (numeric digits `0`–`9`, `{{key:ENTER}}`, `{{key:+}}`, `{{key:-}}`, `{{key:×}}`, `{{key:÷}}`, clear `{{key:C}}`, and backspace `{{key:<-}}`).
- **`{{key:f}}` Shift Key (Row 5, Col 0):** Pressing `{{key:f}}` first unlocks the secret superpower printed directly above the keycap. This includes tools like finding common multiples with `{{key:LCM}}`, simplifying fractions step-by-step with `{{key:SIMP}}`, unit conversions, and memory register recall `{{key:REGS}}`.
- **`{{key:g}}` Shift Key (Row 6, Col 0):** Pressing `{{key:g}}` first unlocks the secret superpower printed on or below the keycap. This includes prime factor trees with `{{key:FACT}}`, skip-counting replication with `{{key:REP}}`, integer division with remainder `{{key:÷R}}`, low-power standby `{{key:OFF}}`, and hardware settings `{{key:FLAGS}}`.

## Built Like a Tank: How Your Calculator Survives the Classroom

Classroom tools must endure energetic daily life: slipping off 30-inch classroom desks onto hard floors, tumbling in backpacks packed with heavy metal binders, and surviving custodial wipe-downs.

The SC-6 is built around four physical principles of durability:

### What Hides Inside a Clicking Button?
Beneath every keycap rests a tiny arch of spring-tempered stainless steel, shaped like a microscopic umbrella. When your finger pushes down on the dished keycap, the metal arch resists until you reach exactly 400 grams of downward force. At that precise instant, the dome buckles downward with a crisp, audible *snap*, bridging two gold-plated electrical contacts on the circuit board below.

You feel that snap instantly in your fingertip and hear a gentle *click*. You never have to wonder, *Did the calculator hear me?* Your finger felt the number commit. As soon as you lift your finger, the steel arch springs back to its resting shape in less than two milliseconds, ready for your next number. This mechanical feedback completely eliminates the double-taps and skipped strokes common to flimsy rubber calculators.

### Why Your Screen Works Like Paper Instead of a Flashlight
Most phone and tablet screens are like tiny lightbulbs: they shine bright electric light directly into your eyes, causing pupils to constrict and delicate eye muscles to tire after twenty minutes of homework.

The SC-6 uses a transflective dot-matrix screen that produces zero light of its own ($0.00\text{ mW/sr}\cdot\text{m}^2$). Instead, it operates just like the printed paper page of this book. Ambient daylight enters through the protective front glass, passes through the liquid crystal numerals, strikes a mirror-like reflector on the back wall of the screen, and bounces straight back out to your eyes. The brighter the classroom or the sunshine on the patio, the crisper and blacker the numbers appear! You can explore math for hours without a hint of optical strain.

### The Sliding 15° Desk Stand: Built-in Easel
Slide the protective cover downward off the front face of the calculator. Turn the calculator over and examine the back: two precision dovetail guide rails run along the outer edges of the chassis. Slide the cowl upward along those rails until a gentle snap locks it firmly in place.

Set the instrument down on your desk. The SC-6 now tilts toward your eyes at an exact $15^\circ$ reading angle—just like a miniature drafting easel! Both of your hands remain completely free: your left hand can build a fraction tower or arrange counters in a ten-frame, while your right hand taps numbers into the stack. When math time concludes, slide the cowl off the back, slide it over the front glass display, and place the calculator into your backpack. The screen is armor-plated against metal binder rings, rulers, and compass points.

### Secret White Bubbles: Why the Letters Never Rub Off
Traditional school calculators print their numbers and symbols with surface ink. Within a few months of daily math practice, your fingers wear off the numbers—leaving blank keys right when you need them most.

The SC-6 completely eliminates surface ink. Instead, every number and symbol is permanently created *inside* the keycap using **1064nm Infrared Laser Micro-Foaming**:

## What to Do If You Get Stuck (Troubleshooting Pathways)

### Screen Is Dark or Blank
- **Check 1 (Sleep Mode):** The SC-6 automatically goes to sleep after 3 minutes to save power. Tap `{{key:C}}` once—the screen wakes instantly in under 5 milliseconds!
- **Check 2 (Room Light):** Remember, your daylight screen produces zero blue light and works by reflecting the light in your room. If the room is dark, turn on a lamp or move near a window.
- **Check 3 (Battery Check):** If tapping `{{key:C}}` under bright light does not wake the screen, ask an adult to inspect the CR2032 coin cell battery.

### Returning to Normal Numbers from a Sandbox Mode
If your screen is showing clock dials (`{{key:🕒}}`), ten-frames (`{{key:▦}}`), or number lines (`{{key:LINE}}`), and you want standard numbers back, choose any of these three easy pathways:
1. **The Universal Clear Key:** Tap `{{key:C}}` once or twice. When no digits are actively being typed, `{{key:C}}` instantly returns to Regular Number Mode (`{{key:NUM}}`).
2. **The Mode Toggle:** Tap the active mode button again (if in `{{key:▦}}`, tap `{{key:▦}}` again).
3. **The Format Keys:** Tap any number format key (`{{key:FIX}}`, `{{key:ALL}}`, or `{{key:FDISP}}`) to exit the visual tool immediately.

#### Problem 1.1: Battery Reserve Life on the Kitchen Table
Inside the rear tool-secured bayonet hatch of your SC-6 sits a single CR2032 lithium coin cell with a reserve capacity of $225\text{ mAh}$ (backed by non-volatile memory that keeps your stack registers and quiz scores safe even during a battery swap). During an active school day, your calculator runs for $2\text{ hours}$ in active classroom calculation mode (drawing $0.65\text{ mA}$) and spends the remaining $22\text{ hours}$ in deep sleep mode (drawing just $0.0025\text{ mA}$). How much battery capacity does it use in a full day, and how many school days will a single coin cell last?

To find out on the stack (`{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_01:keys}}` $\rightarrow$ **`{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_01:value}}`**), first multiply the active draw (`0.65 {{key:ENTER}} 2 {{key:×}}` $\to 1.30\text{ mAh}$), then multiply the sleep draw (`0.0025 {{key:ENTER}} 22 {{key:×}}` $\to 0.055\text{ mAh}$, while $1.30$ waits on Floor $Y$), and tap `{{key:+}}` to combine them into $1.355\text{ mAh/day}$. Finally, type the $225\text{ mAh}$ total capacity, tap `{{key:x⇄y}}` so $225$ sits on Floor $Y$ above $1.355$ on Floor $X$, and press `{{key:÷}}` to reveal **`166.05` school days**—nearly an entire school year on one tiny coin cell!

#### Problem 1.2: Screen Memory (Dot-Matrix Frame Buffer)
Look closely at your SC-6's transflective daylight screen: it is a crisp grid of $132$ columns by $65$ rows of tiny square pixels that draw ten-frames, number-line jump arcs, and bold numbers without needing a battery-draining backlight. Each pixel uses 1 bit of memory (`1` for ink-black, `0` for silver-white), and $8\text{ bits}$ pack into $1\text{ byte}$ of RAM. How many bytes of memory does it take to hold one full screen picture? Key in `{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_02:keys}}` $\rightarrow$ **`{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_02:value}}`**: multiplying `132 {{key:ENTER}} 65 {{key:×}}` finds all $8,580\text{ pixels}$ in Register $X$, and tapping `8 {{key:÷}}` packs those bits into **`1072.5` bytes** ($1,073\text{ whole bytes}$ of screen memory) with $(T: 1, Z: 1, Y: 0, X: 1072.5)$.

#### Problem 1.3: Daylight Transflective Screen Contrast
Why does your SC-6 screen look *sharper* when you sit by a sunny window instead of washing out like a tablet? Because ambient room light bounces off the silver reflector behind the liquid crystal! Under bright classroom lighting ($500\text{ lux}$), a light meter measures the bright background at $142.0\text{ cd/m}^2$ and the dark ink pixels at $9.35\text{ cd/m}^2$. To check whether the screen beats the $15:1$ AAA readability standard, key in `{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_03:keys}}` $\rightarrow$ **`{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_03:value}}`**: `142 {{key:ENTER}}` lifts the background brightness to Floor $Y$, `9.35` enters the dark-pixel reading on Floor $X$, and `{{key:÷}}` divides them in one stroke to confirm a crisp **`15.19:1`** contrast ratio $(T: 0, Z: 0, Y: 0, X: 15.1872)$!

#### Problem 1.4: Clicking Button Snap-Dome Spring Rate
Every time you press a button on the SC-6, a tiny stainless-steel snap-dome underneath clicks with $400.0\text{ grams}$ of force over a $1.6\text{ mm}$ downward travel—preventing accidental double-taps and letting your fingers feel each number lock onto the stack. Engineers measure a switch's crispness by its spring rate $k = F \div \Delta x$ in grams per millimeter. Key in `{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_04:keys}}` $\rightarrow$ **`{{res:sc6_01_hardware_architecture_seq_04:value}}`**: this sequence places the $400.0\text{ g}$ force on Floor $Y$, divides by the $1.6\text{ mm}$ travel on Floor $X$, and lands on an exact spring rate of **`250.0` g/mm** $(T: 0, Z: 0, Y: 3756, X: 250)$.

Finally, `3 {{key:ENTER}}` lifts $5$ to Floor $Z$, `2 {{key:×}}` computes the Counting Box ($6\text{ units}$), and `{{key:+}}` sums the whole station to **`11` Gridfinity squares**!
