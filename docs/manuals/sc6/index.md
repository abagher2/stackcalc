### The Equals-Sign Trap vs. The Stack Ladder
Decades of educational research in early mathematics education (Jerome Bruner {{cite:bruner1966}}, Zoltan Dienes {{cite:dienes1960}}, and Liping Ma {{cite:ma1999}}) identify the "equals-sign trap" as the single greatest cognitive stumbling block transitioning from elementary arithmetic to pre-algebra. In typical elementary classrooms, students see `=` at the end of every line on a worksheet. Consequently, children come to view the equals sign not as a relational statement of balance, but as an urgent command meaning *"produce the answer right now!"*

When these students later encounter algebraic expressions such as:
$$8 + 4 = \Box + 5$$
an alarming majority of students write $12$ in the box (or $17$), because their eyes lock onto $8 + 4$ and treat the equals sign as an immediate trigger to compute {{cite:strogatz2012}}. 

The StackCalc SC-6 dismantles this trap by replacing the equals sign with the **Stack Ladder**. There is no `[=]` button on the faceplate. Instead, mathematical quantities sit as physical objects on visible shelves:
1. **Quantity First:** You pick up an object or measure a quantity onto the active shelf ($X$).
2. **The Elevator Lift:** Tapping `{{key:ENTER}}` lifts that object smoothly onto the upper mezzanine shelf ($Y$).
3. **Second Quantity:** You bring another quantity into contact on the ground shelf ($X$).
4. **Operational Action:** Tapping an operator (`{{key:+}}`, `{{key:-}}`, `{{key:×}}`, `{{key:÷}}`) causes the upper shelf to drop down, fusing the objects into a new composite quantity immediately.

On the Stack Ladder, computation is never an opaque command—it is a tangible running balance of physical objects that can be grouped, scaled, and inspected at will.

### Three Foolproof Ways to Return to Regular Number Mode
Students and teachers never get "trapped" inside a visual mode. The SC-6 provides three foolproof, frictionless pathways to return immediately to standard numbers:
1. **Universal `{{key:C}}` (Clear / Cancel) Key:** Pressing `{{key:C}}` (Row 7, Col 1) when no digits are actively being typed instantly dismisses any active visual mode and restores Regular Number Mode (`{{key:NUM}}`).
2. **Mode Key Toggle:** Tapping the active mode key a second time acts as an immediate toggle back to Regular Number Mode (e.g. in `{{key:▦}}`, pressing `{{key:▦}}` returns to numbers).
3. **Number Display Keys (`{{key:FIX}}`, `{{key:ALL}}`, `{{key:FDISP}}`):** Pressing any standard numerical formatting key immediately exits visual graphics and renders the active stack in that numerical format.

**Strict Data Preservation Guarantee:** Switching between visual modes and number mode is **100% non-destructive**. The stack registers ($X, Y, Z, T$), `LAST x`, and continuous memory are never altered. Visual modes are simply alternative optical lenses for viewing the exact same underlying RPN stack values.

#### Misconception 1: The "Confirm with Clear" Reflex
- **Student Behavior:** The child keys `12`, then habitually taps `{{key:C}}` because on a smartphone or cash register, `C` is sometimes confused with "Continue" or "Confirm." Register $X$ resets to `0`, and the child complains that the calculator "lost" their number.
- **Root Cause:** Conflation of Clear with Enter.
- **Teacher Script:** *"Remember, on StackCalc, the big {{key:ENTER}} key is like the elevator doors closing. It locks your number into the elevator so it can ride upstairs to Floor Y. The {{key:C}} key is the red emergency button that sends everyone out of the building. Use {{key:ENTER}} when you want to keep your number!"*

#### Misconception 2: Decimal Hours vs. Clock Minutes
- **Student Behavior:** When trying to enter 2 hours and 30 minutes, a Grade 4 student types `2.5` because they know that 30 minutes is "half an hour." When they switch to `{{key:🕒}}`, the screen displays `2:50:00` (2 hours and 50 minutes).
- **Root Cause:** Conflating decimal fraction of an hour ($0.5\text{ hr}$) with clock hours, minutes, and seconds notation (`H.MMSS`).
- **Remediation:** 
  1. Have the student observe the difference: in `{{key:🕒}}` mode, `2.30` translates directly to `2:30:00`.
  2. If they have `2.50` decimal hours and want colon time, press `{{key:▸HR}}` to convert between decimal hours and `H.MMSS` automatically!

#### Misconception 3: The Order of Subtraction & Division
- **Student Behavior:** In evaluating $15 - 8$, the student keys `8 {{key:ENTER}} 15 {{key:-}}` and gets `-7`.
- **Root Cause:** In traditional reading, they saw "subtract 8 from 15" and typed 8 first.
- **Teacher Script:** *"Look at the horizontal screen. The left column (Y) is what you have in your basket first. The right column (X) is what you take away. If you have 15 apples and take away 8, the 15 must go into the left column first: 15 {{key:ENTER}} 8 {{key:-}}!"*
- **Quick Fix Keystroke:** If the student enters them backward (`8 {{key:ENTER}} 15`), tell them: *"Do not re-type! Just tap {{key:x⇄y}} to swap their columns, then press {{key:-}}!"*
