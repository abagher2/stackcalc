    12.1 Seeing the Invisible: Why One 64-Point Curve Reveals What a Thousand Numbers Hide

    In the bitter winter of 1637, René Descartes sat inside a stove-heated room in the Netherlands, contemplating a profound schism that had divided natural philosophers since antiquity. Geometry belonged to space—to lines, triangles, and physical bodies drawn with straightedge and compass. Algebra, by contrast, belonged to symbolic computation—to silent numbers, polynomial balances, and abstract equations. Descartes' radical insight in La Géométrie was that these two worlds were identical: every algebraic relation \(y = f(x)\) describes a living geometric curve in space, and every curve is an infinite continuum of algebraic points.

    Centuries later, an engineer wrestling with complex systems—an oscillating LC circuit, an aerodynamic shockwave boundary, or the decay envelope of an underdamped suspension—faces the inverse dilemma. A stack of discrete numbers on a handheld calculator, no matter how precise, cannot immediately reveal a high-frequency resonance peak, a hidden vertical asymptote, or a saddle point. To see the dynamics of a physical system, one must see its shape.

    The StackCalc SC-32 bridges this gap through its dedicated 64-point vector graphics engine ([↱] [PLOT]). By transforming the transflective dot-matrix LCD into a miniature Cartesian laboratory, the instrument evaluates 64 discrete mathematical slices across any domain and renders an interpolated continuous curve in under 42 milliseconds. Paired with interactive crosshair reticle tracing, the engineer can walk along the mathematical terrain, reading exact coordinate pairs from the real-time status bar and committing discovered extrema or inflection points directly back into the RPN operational stack with a single keystroke.

    12.2 The Transflective 64-Point Vector Rendering Engine

    While the StackCalc SC-32 is engineered primarily as a rapid tactile numerical instrument, its \(132 \times 65\) transflective monochrome dot-matrix display features a dedicated 64-Point Vector Graphics Engine ([↱] [PLOT]), accessed via Right Shift [↱] on the multi-action [x∥y] key (Row 7, Column 3), as specified in the HP32SII Deviation Rubric.

    12.2.1 Viewport Geometry & Pixel Mapping

    The physical display is partitioned into two specialized zones: * Graphing Viewport (\(128 \times 52\text{ pixels}\)): World coordinates \((x, y)\) are projected onto screen pixel coordinates \((u, v)\) via linear transformation: $\(u = \left\lfloor \frac{x - X_{\min}}{X_{\max} - X_{\min}} \times 127 \right\rfloor\)$ $\(v = 51 - \left\lfloor \frac{y - Y_{\min}}{Y_{\max} - Y_{\min}} \times 51 \right\rfloor\)$ where \(u \in [0, 127]\) maps horizontally from left to right, and \(v \in [0, 51]\) maps vertically from top to bottom. * Status Readout Line (\(132 \times 13\text{ pixels}\)): The lower 13 vertical pixels (\(v \in [52, 64]\)) are reserved for coordinate text readouts, equation labels, active mode indicators, and crosshair reticle telemetry rendered in high-contrast monospaced font.

    12.2.2 Vector Line Rasterization & Bresenham Algorithm

    Connecting 64 discrete sample points into a continuous graphic curve requires integer line interpolation. The SC-32 graphics pipeline executes a hardware-accelerated Bresenham Vector Line Algorithm directly into the 1,072-byte display frame buffer. Given two adjacent plotted coordinates \((u_0, v_0)\) and \((u_1, v_1)\): $\(\Delta u = |u_1 - u_0|, \quad \Delta v = |v_1 - v_0|\)$ The error term \(\epsilon = 2\Delta v - \Delta u\) steps across pixel increments without floating-point division, drawing smooth 1-pixel wide line segments that preserve high-frequency crests and steep asymptotes.

    12.2.3 Automatic Domain Scaling

    When a mathematical function is plotted: 1. The engine evaluates the target equation at 64 discrete sample points evenly spaced across the domain \([X_{\min}, X_{\max}]\): $\(x_k = X_{\min} + k \left( \frac{X_{\max} - X_{\min}}{63} \right), \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, 63\)$ 2. The internal min/max search algorithm determines \(Y_{\min} = \min(f(x_k))\) and \(Y_{\max} = \max(f(x_k))\). 3. If \(Y_{\max} - Y_{\min} < 10^{-12}\) (a flat constant line), the range automatically pads to \([y - 1, y + 1]\). 4. If the coordinate origin \((0, 0)\) falls within the window, coordinate axes and zero-tick marks are automatically rendered as subtle dotted lines. 5. Total plot generation and rasterization completes in under \(42\text{ milliseconds}\) on the 150 MHz dual-core processor.

    1. Concrete (The Visual Curve): The physical LCD displays the continuous curve trajectory, revealing zeros, asymptotic singularities, local maxima, and harmonic overshoots at a glance.

    3. Abstract (The Stack Commitment): Pressing [ENTER] copies the traced coordinates into the operational stack (\(Y = x, X = y\)), allowing immediate numerical root-finding, integration, or differentiation.

    12.5 Engineering Design Briefing: Real-Time Telemetry Curve Tracing (Problems 12.1 through 12.38)

    The Algebraic Parenthesis Trap vs. The RPN Stack Shortcut: Multi-Point Vector Graphing & Parameter Sweeping

    The Algebraic Parenthesis Trap: On an algebraic graphing calculator, defining an integrand, Fourier harmonic series, or frequency response curve requires entering lengthy strings of nested infix parentheses into a cramped formula editor *([Strogatz, 2019](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-strogatz2019); [Dijkstra, 1961](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-dijkstra1961))*:

    Y1 = ( 1 / ( 1 + ( 2 * 3.14159 * X * 0.001 * 0.0000001 ) ^ 2 ) ^ 0.5 )

    Every evaluation point across the domain re-parses this tokenized string, allocating operator stacks and incurring memory churn. If an engineer forgets a single closing parenthesis or mistypes an exponent grouping, the plotting engine halts with an ambiguous SYNTAX ERROR mid-render, leaving a blank viewport and zero diagnostic insight *([Parker, 2019](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-parker2019); [Ellenberg, 2014](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-ellenberg2014))*.

    The RPN Stack Shortcut: On the StackCalc SC-32, function definitions are written in pure postfix Reverse Polish Notation or evaluated as live stack transforms *([HP, 1982](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-hp15c1982); [HP, 1991](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-hp32sii1991))*. The 64-point vector engine evaluates expressions from the inside out with zero parenthesis overhead. Because each evaluation directly exercises the four hardware stack registers ($X, Y, Z, T$) with deterministic single-cycle execution, total 64-point sampling and Bresenham rasterization completes in under 42 milliseconds. Tapping [ENTER] while tracing commits the exact physical coordinates $(x, y)$ back into Registers $Y$ and $X$, allowing immediate chaining into Brent's root solver ([↱] [SOLVE]) or Adaptive Romberg Quadrature ([↱] [∫]) with zero re-entry *([Brent, 1973](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-brent1973); [Romberg, 1955](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-romberg1955))*.

    12.6.1 Goodness-of-Fit & Coefficient of Determination (\(R^2\))

    The correlation coefficient \(r\) provided by [ŷ] quantifies the linear association between the transformed coordinates. The Coefficient of Determination (\(R^2\)) measures the proportion of variance explained by the model:

    \[R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}} = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}\]

    12.7.1 Active Analytical Reticle Tracing & Root-Finding Handoff

    Classic scientific RPN keystroke-programmable calculators (such as the HP-32SII) delivered compact programming but lacked graphic rasterization. Evaluating 64 discrete domain points directly on hardware registers (\(X, Y, Z, T\)) bypasses repetitive tokenization and AST parsing, ensuring instantaneous rendering.     12.8 Diagnostic Checkpoints & Singularity Handling

    Diagnostic Checkpoint 12.1: Domain Range Singularities ($X_{\min} \ge X_{\max}$)

    If you enter $X_{\min} = 10.0$ and $X_{\max} = 10.0$ (or $X_{\min} > X_{\max}$) and press `[↱] [PLOT]`, the calculator alerts `Error 01: INVALID DATA`. The lower domain limit $X_{\min}$ must be strictly less than $X_{\max}$.

    Diagnostic Checkpoint 12.2: Extracting Traced Coordinates to the Stack

    When tracing a curve with `[↓]` and `[↑]`, pressing `[ENTER]` exits plot mode and pushes the active crosshair coordinates onto the operational stack:

    • Register \(Y\) receives the horizontal coordinate \(x\).
    • Register \(X\) receives the vertical evaluated coordinate \(y\). This allows you to immediately perform arithmetic or numerical root-finding on graphically extracted values.

    Diagnostic Checkpoint 12.3: Angular Mode Consistency in Waveform Plotting

    When plotting trigonometric functions such as Fourier series or sinc waves over domains involving $\pi$ ($[-3.14, +3.14]$), ensure the calculator is in **Radians mode (`[RAD]`)**. Plotting over $[-3.14, +3.14]$ while in **Degrees mode (`[DEG]`)** will evaluate only a tiny $3.14^\circ$ sliver of the cycle!

    Diagnostic Checkpoint 12.4: Out-of-Bounds Arithmetic in Plot Loops

    If an equation evaluates to an undefined result (such as $\sqrt{-1}$ without complex mode, or division by zero $\frac{1}{0}$) at one of the 64 sample points, the vector engine flags that point as an open discontinuity and does NOT connect vector lines across the singularity.
