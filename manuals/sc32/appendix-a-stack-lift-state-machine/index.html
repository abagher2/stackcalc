    CHAPTER BASELINE MODE: [ALL] (Full 12-digit scientific mantissa; Standard Base-10 Arithmetic; Degrees angular mode default).

    A.1 Theoretical Foundation of Stack Lift

    In Reverse Polish Notation (RPN), the four operational registers—designated \(X\) (Bottom/Display), \(Y\) (Second), \(Z\) (Third), and \(T\) (Top)—are governed by an autonomous finite state machine known as the Stack Lift Automaton.

    Unlike algebraic calculators that require dynamic operator precedence stacks and recursive parenthesis parsing trees, an RPN architecture maintains execution immediacy: every keystroke maps directly to a deterministic state transition in constant time \(\mathcal{O}(1)\). Rooted in the classic Corvallis engineering specifications of the HP-15C and HP-32SII (HP, 1982; HP, 1991), this state machine eliminates ambiguity while providing complete computational transparency.

    Stack Lift Transition Diagram

    | Current State | Triggering Keystroke Class | Next State | Stack Register Effect | | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **LIFT ARMED** (`Lift = TRUE`) | First digit (`[0]`–`[9]`, `[.]`) | **EDIT BUFFER** | Pushes stack up (`X → Y → Z → T`) before writing new digit | | **EDIT BUFFER** (In Progress) | Any function/operator (`[+]`, `[×]`, `[√x]`) | **LIFT ARMED** (`Lift = TRUE`) | Terminates digit entry, evaluates result into `X`, and arms stack lift | | **EDIT BUFFER** or **LIFT ARMED** | `[ENTER]`, `[CLx]`, `[Σ+]` | **LIFT DISABLED** (`Lift = FALSE`) | Next digit typed will overwrite `X` in place without lifting `Y` | | **LIFT DISABLED** (`Lift = FALSE`) | `[x⇄y]`, `[R↓]`, `[↱] [R↑]`, or operator | **LIFT ARMED** (`Lift = TRUE`) | Re-arms stack lift for subsequent numeric entry |

    A.1.1 The Three State Categories

    At any discrete operational moment, the calculator's internal control register holds a single boolean flag: stackLiftEnabled. This flag dictates the stack's reaction when the user initiates numeric data entry:

    1. Enables Stack Lift (stackLiftEnabled = true):
    2. Subsequent numeric keypresses (digits 0–9, decimal point ., or E) push the entire stack upward before writing the new digit into Register \(X\).
    3. Register movement: \(T \leftarrow Z\), \(Z \leftarrow Y\), \(Y \leftarrow X\), and \(X \leftarrow \text{digit}\).
    4. The former Top register (\(T\)) is discarded off the top of the stack.

    5. This is the default armed state produced by nearly all mathematical functions (+, -, ×, ÷, √x, SIN, LN, etc.) and stack rolls ([R↓], [R↑], [x⇄y]).

    6. Disables Stack Lift (stackLiftEnabled = false):

    7. Subsequent numeric keypresses overwrite Register \(X\) directly without pushing the upper registers upward.
    8. Register movement: \(X \leftarrow \text{digit}\), while \(Y\), \(Z\), and \(T\) remain completely undisturbed.
    9. This state is deliberately established by [ENTER], [CLx] (Clear X), and statistical accumulation [Σ+].

    10. The Pedagogical Invariant: The [ENTER] key duplicates \(X\) into \(Y\) (\(Y \leftarrow X\)) and immediately disables stack lift so that the very next number keyed replaces the duplicate in \(X\) while preserving the operand in \(Y\).

    11. Preserves Stack Lift (stackLiftEnabled unchanged):

    12. Non-operational or modifier keystrokes (such as prefix keys [↰] and [↱], [STO], [+/-], and [E]) leave the state of the stack lift flag unaltered.

    [!NOTE] The Stack Lift Automaton guarantees that intermediate results never need to be explicitly saved or parenthesized. Understanding whether an operation enables or disables stack lift eliminates 100% of operand duplication errors.

    A.4 The Formal Register Delta Algebra

    To verify calculation pipelines with mathematical certainty, the effect of any operator \(\Omega\) on the four-register stack \(\mathbf{S} = [X, Y, Z, T]^T\) is expressed as a matrix or functional transformation \(\mathbf{S}' = \mathbf{f}_\Omega(\mathbf{S})\).

    A.4.1 Unary Operators (\(f(X)\))

    Unary functions include [√x], [1/x], [eˣ], [LN], [SIN], [COS], [TAN], [x²], [10ˣ], [LOG], [x!], and [|x|]: $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} f(X) \\ Y \\ Z \\ T \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = X, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{TRUE}\)$

    A.4.2 Dyadic Operators (\(f(Y, X)\))

    Standard binary operators include [+], [-], [×], [÷], [yˣ], [ˣ√y], and [x∥y]: $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} f(Y, X) \\ Z \\ T \\ T \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = X, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{TRUE}\)$

    [!IMPORTANT] The Top-Register Constant Replication Invariant: Observe that during any dyadic operation, Register \(T\) drops into \(Z\), while \(T\) retains its original value (\(T' \leftarrow T\)). This structural invariant allows a constant stored in \(T\) to be reused indefinitely across an unbounded series of operations without re-entry!

    A.4.3 Circular Stack Rotations

    Rotations rearrange registers without altering numerical values or updating LASTX: * Roll Down ([R↓]): $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ Z \\ T \\ X \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = \text{LASTX}, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{TRUE}\)$ * Roll Up ([↱] [R↑]): $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} T \\ X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = \text{LASTX}, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{TRUE}\)$ * Exchange \(X\) and \(Y\) ([x⇄y]): $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ X \\ Z \\ T \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = \text{LASTX}, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{TRUE}\)$

    A.4.4 Stack Elevator ([ENTER])

    The [ENTER] operation pushes the stack while replicating \(X\) into \(Y\): $\(\begin{bmatrix} X' \\ Y' \\ Z' \\ T' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{LASTX}' = \text{LASTX}, \quad \text{Lift}' = \text{FALSE}\)$

    A.5.12 Trace 12: Dynamic Stack Depth Expansion (4-LVL vs 8-LVL vs INF)

    Under extended system flags ([↱] [FLAGS]), the user can expand the stack beyond 4 levels: * 4-LVL (Classic HP): 4 registers (\(X, Y, Z, T\)). \(T\) replicated on drop; values pushed off \(T\) are discarded. * 8-LVL (Extended STEM): 8 registers (\(X, Y, Z, T, A, B, C, D\)). Deeper parenthetical evaluations can occur without variable storage. \(D\) replicated on drop. * INF (Infinite Dynamic): Dynamic heap allocation. Stack grows unboundedly until system memory limit. Stack drop pulls previous elements down without replication.
