    11.1 The Application: The Time Value of Human Labor & William Kahan's Financial Invariant

    Money is not merely abstract numbers printed on green paper or glowing as digits in a bank ledger. In the physical universe, money represents stored human time and labor.

    When an engineer signs an employment contract, an entrepreneur secures an equipment lease, or a family signs a 30-year home mortgage, the mathematics of compound interest dictates whether that labor compounds into generational freedom or evaporates into perpetual interest debt.

    11.1.1 William Kahan's Crusade: The War on Destructive Percentage Keys

    In the late 1970s, UC Berkeley Professor and future Turing Award laureate William M. Kahan inspected the commercial handheld calculators sold to consumers and business executives. What he discovered appalled him.

    On almost every algebraic calculator of the era, the percentage key was destructive. Suppose you were billing a corporate client for \(\$150.00\) of engineering consulting, plus an \(8.25\%\) state sales tax:

    \[\text{Algebraic keystrokes: } 150 \ [\times] \ 8.25 \ [\%] \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Displays } 12.38\]

    The moment you pressed [↱] [%], the calculator obliterated your \(\$150.00\) base amount! To find the invoice total (\(\$162.38\)), you had to either manually re-enter \(\$150.00\), write it down on scratch paper, or rely on a clunky memory key ([M+]). If the client asked to test an alternative \(9.5\%\) tax rate, you had to start completely over from scratch.

    Kahan declared war on this numerical incompetence. In 1979, writing in the Hewlett-Packard Journal (Kahan, 1979), he formulated what is now celebrated in numerical computing as the Kahan Percentage Invariant:

    The Kahan Percentage Invariant

    A percentage operator must be non-destructive and dyadic:

    $$X \longleftarrow \frac{Y \times X}{100}, \qquad Y \longleftarrow Y \quad (\text{Preserved frozen on the stack!})$$

    On the StackCalc SC-32, your base amount never disappears:

    1. Key in: 150 [ENTER] 8.25 [↱] [%]
      → Register $X$ displays 12.38 (the tax amount).
      → Register $Y$ silently holds 150.00 (the base invoice).
    2. Total Invoice? Just tap [+] → $162.38!
    3. Discount Subtracted? Just tap [-] → $137.62!
    4. Alternative Rate? Tap [↰] [LASTX] [x⇄y] or test alternate taxes directly without re-entry!

    Beyond simple percentages, all commercial banking, corporate bonds, and mortgages rest on the 5-variable Time Value of Money (TVM) equation:

    \[PV + PMT \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-N}}{i} \right] + FV (1 + i)^{-N} = 0\]

    The SC-32's dedicated TVM register suite frees you from the tyranny of opaque amortization software. You control the five dials of capital. )

    Inside-Out Stack Architecture: Algebraic Trap vs. RPN Shortcut — Compound TVM & Amortization Formulas

    The Algebraic Parenthesis Trap: Consider evaluating the compound interest factor or monthly mortgage annuity payment manually from the fundamental closed-form equation:

    $$PMT = PV \cdot \frac{i(1 + i)^N}{(1 + i)^N - 1}$$

    On an algebraic textbook calculator, this formula is a minefield of nested parentheses, cognitive buffering, and operator precedence traps *([Sweller, 1988](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-sweller1988); [Dijkstra, 1961](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-dijkstra1961))*. An analyst must key:

    PV * ( ( I / 1200 ) * ( 1 + I / 1200 ) ^ N ) / ( ( 1 + I / 1200 ) ^ N - 1 )

    Notice the fatal vulnerabilities: ( I / 1200 ) must be recomputed or stored in scratch memory three separate times; five levels of open parentheses must remain balanced in working memory; and a single missing closing parenthesis triggers a syntax error or a silent erroneous amortization table *([Parker, 2019](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-parker2019); [Ellenberg, 2014](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-ellenberg2014))*.

    The RPN Stack Shortcut: On the StackCalc SC-32, the computation proceeds from the inside out using The Inside-Out Growth Factor and The LASTX Dual-Branch Trick *([HP, 1982](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-hp15c1982); [HP, 1991](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-hp32sii1991))*. The periodic interest rate $i = I/1200$ is computed once. Adding 1 yields the base $(1 + i)$. Raising to the $N$-th power yields $G = (1 + i)^N$. Tap [ENTER] to duplicate $G$, subtract 1 to form the denominator $(G - 1)$ in Register $X$, and tap [÷] against the numerator branch or use The x⇄y Denominator Flip. Every intermediate subtotal is physically visible on the transflective dot-matrix LCD, requiring zero parentheses, zero scratchpad re-entry, and zero cognitive load *([Miller, 1956](appendix-e-annotated-bibliography.md#cite-miller1956))*.

    CHAPTER BASELINE MODE: [FIX 2] (Fixed 2-decimal currency display; Standard Base-10 Arithmetic; Degrees angular mode default). TVM variables and four operational stack registers ($X, Y, Z, T$) are maintained continuously in non-volatile hardware memory.

    11.2.1 The Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) Financial Continuum

    Financial comprehension on the SC-32 bridges three distinct pedagogical levels: 1. Concrete (The Cash Ledger): Physical dollar bills exchanged across a commercial transaction table or loan amortization ledger. Every outflow leaves the wallet (represented by negative sign [+/-]), and every inflow enters the wallet (positive sign). 2. Representational (The Operational 4-Stack): The vertical registers (\(X, Y, Z, T\)) represent distinct balance accounts. Register \(X\) represents active liquid cash in hand; Register \(Y\) preserves the pre-tax invoice baseline; Register \(Z\) holds cumulative tax reserves; Register \(T\) retains repeating scaling multipliers. 3. Abstract (The Actuarial Equations): Compound interest, annuity discount factors, and exponential discounting formulas operate behind simple single-key triggers ([↱] [%], [↱] [%CHG], [↰] [TVM], [PMT]).

    On the StackCalc SC-32, the Percentage Function ([↱] [%]) is an invariant dyadic operator adhering strictly to William Kahan's non-destructive specification:

    \[\text{Calculation: } \quad X \leftarrow \frac{Y \times X}{100}, \quad Y \leftarrow Y \text{ (Preserved!)}, \quad \text{Lift} \leftarrow \text{TRUE}\]

In RPN: * 150 [ENTER] 8.25 [↱] [%] puts \(\$12.38\) in \(X\) and preserves \(\$150.00\) in \(Y\). * Pressing [+] yields \(\$162.38\) in one stroke. * Pressing [↰] [LASTX] recovers \(\$12.38\), or rolling the stack allows immediate further analysis.

    11.5 Cross-Metric Elasticity & Spread: The Multi-Metric Stack Advantage

    The true computational superiority of the RPN operational stack emerges during comparative multi-metric corporate analytics.

    Consider an executive evaluating a software portfolio company: * Metric A (Annual Recurring Revenue - ARR): Grew from \(\$12.0\text{M}\) to \(\$15.6\text{M}\). * Metric B (Customer Acquisition Cost - CAC): Expanded from \(\$2.4\text{M}\) to \(\$2.88\text{M}\).

    The executive must determine: 1. The ARR vs. CAC Growth Spread: The percentage point difference in expansion rates (\(\Delta \text{ARR}\% - \Delta \text{CAC}\%\)). 2. The Growth Elasticity Ratio: The sensitivity of revenue growth relative to acquisition spending (\(\Delta \text{ARR}\% \div \Delta \text{CAC}\%\)).

    Register \(T\) \(0.00\) Top of stack —

    Register \(Y\) \(+30.00\%\) Metric A YoY Growth (\(\Delta\text{ARR}\%\)) Base comparator

    11.6 The 5-Variable Time Value of Money (TVM) Architecture

    The SC-32 features an onboard Five-Variable Time Value of Money (TVM) Engine accessed via Left Shift [↰] followed by [↰] [TVM] on the multi-action [x∥y] key (Row 7, Column 3), as specified in the HP32SII Deviation Rubric.

    TVM Variable LFU Softkey Actuarial Description Computational Units
    \(N\) [N] Total number of compounding periods Integer or fractional periods
    \(I\%YR\) [I%YR] Annual nominal interest rate Percent per year (e.g. 6.5 for 6.5%)
    \(PV\) [PV] Present value (initial loan principal or investment) Currency ($)
    \(PMT\) [PMT] Periodic annuity payment amount Currency ($ per period)
    \(FV\) [FV] Future value (final balloon balance or accumulated savings) Currency ($)

    11.6.1 The Master Actuarial TVM Equation

    The five financial variables are bound by the universal compound interest annuity formula:

    \[PV + PMT \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-N}}{i} \right] (1 + i \cdot S) + FV (1 + i)^{-N} = 0\]

    where: * \(i = \frac{I\%YR}{100 \times P/\text{YR}}\) is the periodic interest rate per compounding period. * \(N\) is the total integer count of compounding periods. * \(S = 0\) for End-of-Period payments (Ordinary Annuity, default). * \(S = 1\) for Beginning-of-Period payments (Annuity Due, flagged by the BEGIN annunciator).

    11.6.2 Strict Cash Flow Sign Conventions

    • Cash Inflow (\(+\)): Money received into your hands/account (e.g., bank loans, proceeds, receipts).
    • Cash Outflow (\(-\), [+/-]): Money paid out from your pocket (e.g., initial investment deposits, mortgage payments, maintenance costs).

    11.9 Teaching & Instrument Architecture Notes

    11.9.1 Register Y Preservation During Percentage Operations

    A classic pitfall in percentage arithmetic on algebraic calculators is keying 100 + 8% = and receiving an unintended output like 100.08. On the SC-32, executing 100 [ENTER] 8 [↱] [%] places the exact percentage value (\(8.00\)) in Register \(X\) while freezing the base (\(100.00\)) in Register \(Y\). Both quantities remain visible simultaneously on the stack before committing to addition ([+] for tax or markup) or subtraction ([-] for discount). Similarly, economic price elasticity \(\frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta P}\) is evaluated directly by stacking the two percentage changes into Registers \(Y\) and \(X\) and executing [÷].

    11.9.2 Actuarial Sign Discipline & Amortization Mechanics

    The five-variable TVM solver operates on strict actuarial conservation of cash flow: $\(PV + PMT \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-N}}{i} \right] + FV (1 + i)^{-N} = 0\)$ Every cash transaction is directional: cash inflows received are entered as positive values, while cash outflows disbursed (investments, down payments, periodic amortizations) are entered as negative values via [+/-]. If all inputs share the same sign, the solver flags an actuarial contradiction (ERROR 5: NO SOLUTION). Comparing a 30-year versus 15-year fixed mortgage illustrates the power of compounding: on a \(\$350,000\) mortgage at \(6.75\%\), the 30-year schedule accumulates over \(\$467,000\) in interest, whereas a 15-year term saves over \(\$277,300\) by accelerating principal amortization.

    11.10 Diagnostic Checkpoints & Singularity Handling

    [!CHECKPOINT] Diagnostic Checkpoint 11.1: Cash Flow Sign Error (ERROR 5: NO SOLUTION)
    If all TVM inputs are entered with the same sign (e.g., \(PV = 1000, PMT = 50, FV = 1000\)), the compound interest solver halts with ERROR 5: NO SOLUTION. Actuarial balance requires at least one cash inflow (\(+\)) and one cash outflow (\(-\), entered via [+/-]).

    [!CHECKPOINT] Diagnostic Checkpoint 11.2: Periodic Rate Conversion Consistency
    When solving monthly mortgages (\(N = 360\)), the annual nominal interest rate \(I\%YR\) must be divided by 12 before storing into [I%YR]. Storing the full annual percentage (\(6.75\%\)) directly would imply a crippling \(6.75\%\) monthly interest rate (\(81.0\%\text{ APR}\))!

    [!CHECKPOINT] Diagnostic Checkpoint 11.3: Payment Mode Annunciator (BEGIN vs. END)
    If mortgage payment calculations differ slightly from bank quotes (e.g., \(\$2,257\) vs \(\$2,270\)), verify whether the BEGIN annunciator is lit on the LCD. Mortgages pay at the END of each month; leases and college tuition pay at the BEGINning. Toggle via [↰] [TVM][BEG/END].

    [!CHECKPOINT] Diagnostic Checkpoint 11.4: Base Preservation in Register Y
    Remember that after executing [Base] [ENTER] [Rate] [↱] [%], Register \(Y\) still contains [Base]. Pressing [+] adds the percentage to the base, while pressing [-] subtracts it. If you wish to retain the computed percentage in \(X\) and discard the base, press [x⇄y] [R↓].
