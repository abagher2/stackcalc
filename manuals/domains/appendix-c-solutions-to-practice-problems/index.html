Solution: Evaluates rated secondary current \(I_{\text{FLA}} = 1{,}500{,}000 / (\sqrt{3} \times 480) = \mathbf{1{,}804.22\text{ A}}\), and computes short-circuit current \(I_{\text{sc}} = 1804.22 / 0.0575 = \mathbf{31{,}377.73\text{ A}}\) (requiring \(35\text{ kA}\) or \(65\text{ kA}\) rated switchgear).

    Unit III (SC-16): Digital Systems, Bitwise Logic & Embedded Timing

    Chapter 7: The Stuck Interrupt Flag in the Motor Controller

    1. Two's Complement Additive Inversion
    2. Keystrokes: [f] [DEC] 8 [g] [WSZ] 42 [f] [NEG] [HEX] 42 [f] [DEC] [+]
    3. Solution: Sets 8-bit word size and negates to yield \(-42_{10} = \mathbf{\text{0xD6}}\) in \(X\). Adding \(+42\) produces modular overflow to \(\mathbf{\text{0x00}}\), confirming the two's complement additive inverse.
    4. Contiguous Bitmask Generation
    5. Keystrokes: [HEX] 16 [g] [WSZ] 6 [ENTER] 9 [f] [MSK]
    6. Solution: Generates a 6-bit contiguous mask shifted left by 9 bit positions to return \((2^6 - 1) \ll 9 = 63 \ll 9 = \mathbf{\text{0x7E00}}\) in \(X\).
    7. Logical vs. Arithmetic Right Shift
    8. Keystrokes: [HEX] 16 [g] [WSZ] C000 [ENTER] 3 [≫] [f] [DEC] C000 [HEX] 3 [f] [ASR] [f] [DEC]
    9. Solution: Logical right shift shifts in zero bits, returning \(\mathbf{8{,}192}\) (0x1800, corrupting the sign). Arithmetic right shift replicates the sign bit, returning exactly \(\mathbf{-2{,}048}\) (0xF800, \(-16{,}384 / 8 = -2{,}048\)).
    10. Cortex-M Bit-Band Alias Address Calculation
    11. Keystrokes: [HEX] 32 [g] [WSZ] 40020014 [ENTER] 40000000 [-] 32 [f] [DEC] [×] 3 [ENTER] 4 [×] [+] 42000000 [HEX] [+]
    12. Solution: Calculates byte offset 0x20014 (\(131{,}092_{10}\)), multiplies by 32, adds bit word offset \(3 \times 4 = 12\), and adds alias base 0x42000000 to yield alias address \(\mathbf{\text{0x4240028C}}\).
    13. Q8.8 Fixed-Point Voltage Threshold Encoding
    14. Keystrokes: [f] [DEC] 16 [g] [WSZ] 3.625 [ENTER] 256 [×] [HEX]
    15. Solution: Multiplies floating-point voltage by scaling factor \(2^8 = 256\) to yield \(3.625 \times 256 = 928_{10} = \mathbf{\text{0x03A0}}\) in \(X\) (integer part 0x03 = \(3\text{ V}\), fraction 0xA0/256 = \(0.625\text{ V}\)).

    Chapter 8: The Corrupt Sensor Packet Across the Serial Bus

    1. IEEE-754 Single-Precision Float Encoding
    2. Keystrokes: [f] [DEC] 12.5 [f] [NEG] [FLT]
    3. Solution: Decomposes \(-12.50 = -1.1001_2 \times 2^3\) into sign \(s=1\), biased exponent \(e = 3 + 127 = 130\) (0x82), and mantissa fraction 0x480000 to assemble binary word \(\mathbf{\text{0xC1480000}}\) in \(X\).
    4. 32-Bit Endianness Reversal
    5. Keystrokes: [HEX] 32 [g] [WSZ] 00409642 [g] [SWP]
    6. Solution: Executes 32-bit byte reversal, transposing byte sequence \([B_0, B_1, B_2, B_3] \to [B_3, B_2, B_1, B_0]\) to return \(\mathbf{\text{0x42964000}}\) (\(+75.125^\circ\text{C}\) in IEEE-754).
    7. CIDR /28 Subnet Sizing
    8. Keystrokes: [HEX] 32 [g] [WSZ] FFFFFFFF [ENTER] 4 [≪] [f] [DEC] 2 [ENTER] 4 [f] [yˣ] 2 [-]
    9. Solution: Shifts 32 ones left by \(32 - 28 = 4\) bits to yield subnet mask \(\mathbf{\text{0xFFFFFFF0}}\) (255.255.255.240). Total block is \(2^4 = 16\) IPs, yielding \(16 - 2 = \mathbf{14\text{ usable hosts}}\).
    10. 16x Oversampled UART Baud Divisor on an 80 MHz Clock
    11. Keystrokes: 80000000 [ENTER] 16 [ENTER] 921600 [×] [÷] 5 [ENTER] 16 [×] 80000000 [x⇄y] [÷] 921600 [-] 921600 [÷] 100 [×]
    12. Solution: Calculates ideal divisor \(\text{BRR} = 80\times 10^6 / (16 \times 921{,}600) = \mathbf{5.4253}\). Rounding to integer divisor 5 yields actual baud \(1{,}000{,}000\text{ bps}\), producing an intolerable error of \(\mathbf{+8.51\%}\).
    13. Reflected CRC-32 Verification of a 4-Byte Payload
    14. Keystrokes: [HEX] 32 [g] [WSZ] 12345678 [ENTER] 32 [f] [CRC]
    15. Solution: Executes reflected polynomial division over Galois Field \(GF(2)\) using generator polynomial 0xEDB88320, returning standard Ethernet/ZIP frame check sequence \(\mathbf{\text{0xDF8A8A2B}}\).

    Chapter 9: Partitioning the Factory Floor Subnet and ADC Front End

    1. Precision Sixteen-Bit ADC Step Resolution and Code Inversion
    2. Keystrokes: 2.5 [ENTER] 65536 [÷] 1000000 [×] 35904 [ENTER] 2.5 [×] 65536 [÷]
    3. Solution: Calculates quantization step resolution \(V_{\text{LSB}} = 2.50 / 65536 = \mathbf{38.147\ \mu\text{V}}\) in \(X\). Reconstructing analog input voltage yields \(V_{\text{in}} = (35{,}904 / 65536) \times 2.50 = \mathbf{1.36975\text{ V}}\).
    4. CIDR Subnet Capacity and Broadcast Address Calculation
    5. Keystrokes: 32 [ENTER] 22 [-] 2 [x⇄y] [f] [yˣ] 2 [-]
    6. Solution: Calculates host address space \(2^{32-22} = 2^{10} = \mathbf{1{,}024\text{ IPs}}\), yielding \(1024 - 2 = \mathbf{1{,}022\text{ usable hosts}}\). Broadcast address is \(\mathbf{10.240.19.255}\) (0x0AF013FF).
    7. Cache-Line Descriptor Alignment and Power-of-Two Ring Sizing
    8. Keystrokes: 128 [ENTER] 48 [×] 64 [ENTER] 6144 [x⇄y] [÷]
    9. Solution: Calculates total raw buffer footprint \(128 \times 48 = \mathbf{6{,}144\text{ bytes}}\). Because \(6144 / 64 = 96.00\) divides evenly, zero padding bytes are required, aligning naturally to cache-line boundaries.
    10. High-Speed Synchronous SPI Bus Setup Timing Margin
    11. Keystrokes: 20 [ENTER] 12 [-] 2.5 [-] 6 [-]
    12. Solution: Calculates remaining setup slack margin \(T_{\text{slack}} = 20.0 - (12.0 + 2.5 + 6.0) = 20.0 - 20.5 = \mathbf{-0.50\text{ ns}}\), revealing a severe timing violation requiring a reduced bus clock.
    13. PWM Ultrasonic Auto-Reload Period Sizing
    14. Keystrokes: 120000000 [ENTER] 2 [ENTER] 20000 [×] [÷]
