    RPN Elementary Power Guide

    4-Stack XYZT, Skip-Counting [REP], LastX Verification & Integer Division

    RPN PEDAGOGY // ELEMENTARY FOUNDATIONS (GRADES K–6) ● DEFAULT 4-STACK XYZT • CLASSROOM PROVEN
    📖 Master Guide ⚡ RPN Power Guide 📝 120 Station Worksheets 🎯 Standards Matrix 🧑‍🏫 Teacher Guide 🏆 UIL Competition

    Traditional elementary calculators force children into an algebraic syntax that creates unnecessary cognitive barriers: students struggle with hidden memory registers (M+), syntax errors caused by nested parentheses, and decimal answers when they are learning division with remainders.

    In the latest system revisions, the StackCalc SC-6 Elementary Calculator standardizes on a default 4-stack XYZT live operational display for general arithmetic, while providing an automatic 2-stack mode (X, Y) for paired concrete manipulatives (such as rekenrek counting racks and two-dice probability tables). Users and teachers can also toggle back to 2-stack mode whenever desired. This guide explains the mathematical and pedagogical rationale behind this architecture, and provides classroom-ready teaching modules for core RPN elementary capabilities:

    1. The 4-Stack XYZT Visual Model & 2-Stack Manipulative Modes: Visual permanence for multi-step arithmetic, with focused 2-line views for paired entry.
    2. Integer Division with Remainders ([÷R]): Simultaneous quotient (\(X\)) and remainder (\(Y\)) display.
    3. Instant Inverse Verification ([LASTX]): Concrete student self-checking without teacher intervention.
    4. Rhythmic Skip-Counting ([REP]): Auditory and tactile reinforcement of multiplication facts.

    1. Why 4-Stack XYZT is the Default in Elementary Math

    In early prototype testing, a 2-line display was considered for simplicity. However, empirical classroom trials across Grades 3–6 revealed that a 4-level XYZT stack is dramatically more intuitive for young learners for three fundamental reasons:

    +-------------------------------------------------------+
|  HIGH-CONTRAST MONOCHROME LCD (TERMINUS PIXEL FONT)   |
+-------------------------------------------------------+
|  T:        12.0000   <-- Top / Overflow Memory Level  |
|  Z:        35.0000   <-- Suspended Subtotal Level     |
|  Y:         3.0000   <-- Active Remainder / Operand 1 |
|  X:         5.0000   <-- Active Input / Quotient      |
+-------------------------------------------------------+

    1.1 Cognitive Load & Visual Permanence

    On a standard calculator, intermediate calculations vanish. In a problem like: $\((4 \times 8) + (15 \div 3)\)$ a student evaluating \(4 \times 8 = 32\) must either write \(32\) down on scratch paper, memorize it in short-term memory, or master obscure memory keys (STO, RCL, M+).

    On the SC-6, evaluating 4 [ENTER] 8 [×] leaves 32 sitting visibly in the stack. When the student types 15 [ENTER] 3 [÷], 32 gently slides up to Register \(Y\), while 5 appears in Register \(X\). Both numbers remain simultaneously visible on the screen. Pressing [+] (full sequence: 4 [ENTER] 8 [×] 15 [ENTER] 3 [÷] [+]) combines them to yield 37. Zero writing, zero memory keys, zero working memory fatigue.

    1.2 Zero Parentheses Syntax Errors

    Elementary students have not mastered algebraic formal syntax. On standard in-fix calculators, entering: $\(2 + 3 \times 4\)$ yields either 20 (on cheap non-precedence calculators) or 14 (on algebraic hierarchy machines), creating profound confusion about what the machine is actually doing. In RPN, there is strictly no ambiguity and no parentheses keys: * If you mean add first: 2 [ENTER] 3 [+] 4 [×] = 20 * If you mean multiply first: 3 [ENTER] 4 [×] 2 [+] = 14

    The student controls the order of operations explicitly, mirroring the natural mental decomposition taught in Singapore Math and Bridges.

    1.3 Uniformity Across the K–12 Continuum

    By standardizing on a 4-stack XYZT display from Grade 1, students develop an intuitive spatial mental model of the stack that carries forward seamlessly to the flagship SC-32 Scientific Calculator in middle school, high school AP Physics, and collegiate engineering. There is zero unlearning.

    1.4 Two-Stack Mode for Special Manipulative Entries & Stack Depth Rubric

    While the 4-stack XYZT architecture is the default for general multi-step arithmetic, early elementary concrete manipulatives are frequently binary: * Counting Racks & Dual Ten-Frames (Rekenreks): A standard counting rack has two rows of beads (top wire and bottom wire). When using counting rack and ten-frame shelf entry modes ([▦] or [g] [COUNT]), the SC-6 automatically focuses on a Two-Stack Display (\(Y, X\)). The top row maps directly to Register \(Y\) and the bottom row maps to Register \(X\). This creates an exact 1:1 spatial correspondence between the physical beads and the screen without the distraction of upper registers (\(Z, T\)). * Two-Dice Probability & Domino Pairs: Pressing [🎲] generates random rolls directly in a focused 2-stack view, where Die 1 registers in \(Y\) and Die 2 registers in \(X\). Combining them ([+]), finding their difference ([-]), or computing products ([×]) is immediate and uncluttered. * Returning to Standard 4-Stack Number Mode: Pressing [NUM] (Whole Number Mode key at Row 3 Col 4) or [FDISP] immediately exits manipulative mode and restores the default 4-stack XYZT operational display. * Manual Stack Depth Switching ([FLAGS] Key Rubric): Kindergarten and early Grade 1 teachers who want to keep cognitive load minimal across all arithmetic operations can manually switch the SC-6 into 2-stack mode at any time using the hardware configuration rubric: 1. Press [g] [FLAGS] to open the system configuration matrix. 2. Press digit [5] (5:DEP) to cycle stack depth between 4-LV (4-level XYZT) and 2-LV (2-level \(Y, X\)). 3. Press [C] (or [g] [FLAGS]) to dismiss the flags matrix and resume calculations with the chosen stack depth. (In the desktop and companion apps, clicking the LCD stack depth badge or opening the Flags menu provides the same direct toggle.)

    2. Curricular Module 1: Integer Division with Remainders ([÷R])

    // STANDARDS ALIGNMENT
    CCSS-M: 4.OA.A.3, 4.NBT.B.6, 5.NBT.B.6 • TEKS: 4.4E, 4.4F, 5.3C • Singapore: Primary 3A Ch 3, Primary 4A Ch 2

    The Elementary Division Dilemma

    In Grades 3 through 5, division is introduced as fair sharing with leftovers (quotient and remainder): $\(23 \div 4 = 5 \text{ R } 3\)$ When an elementary student enters \(23 \div 4\) on a standard smartphone or cheap school calculator, the screen displays: $\(5.75\)$ For a 9-year-old who has not yet learned decimal place value or fraction-to-decimal conversion, \(5.75\) is actively counter-productive. It fails to answer: * "How many full boxes of 4 can we pack?" (5 boxes) * "How many items are left over?" (3 items, not 0.75 items!)

    Because of this, teachers routinely ban calculators during division units.

    The SC-6 Solution: The [÷R] Integer Division Key

    The SC-6 provides a dedicated Integer Division with Remainder operator located at [g] [÷R] (legend ÷R):

            DIVIDEND                 DIVISOR                     OPERATION
   [2] [3] [ENTER]                 [4]                   [g] [÷R]  (÷R)
          |                         |                          |
          v                         v                          v
+-----------------+       +-----------------+        +-----------------+
| T:         0    |       | T:         0    |        | T:         0    |
| Z:         0    |       | Z:         0    |        | Z:         0    |
| Y:        23    |       | Y:        23    |        | Y:         3  <-- REMAINDER
| X:        23    |       | X:         4    |        | X:         5  <-- QUOTIENT
+-----------------+       +-----------------+        +-----------------+

    Keystroke Protocol & Display State

    1. Enter Dividend: Type 23, press [ENTER].
    2. Enter Divisor: Type 4.
    3. Execute Integer Division: Press [g] [÷R] (full sequence: 23 [ENTER] 4 [g] [÷R]).
    4. Immediate Result:
    5. Register X displays the Quotient (5).
    6. Register Y displays the Remainder (3).
    7. Both values are visible at the same moment on the 4-line display!

    Swapping Quotient and Remainder ([x⇄y])

    If a follow-up calculation requires the remainder, the student does not need to retype it. Simply press [x⇄y] (the primary swap key): * X becomes 3 (Remainder ready for computation) * Y becomes 5 (Quotient preserved in stack)

    Classroom Investigation: Spool Slicing & Filament Leftovers

    Scenario: A 5th-grade maker lab has a 1,000-gram spool of Sunlu Midnight Navy PLA filament. Each 3D-printed calculator housing requires exactly 82 grams. 1. How many complete calculator housings can the class 3D print? 2. How many grams of filament remain on the spool for calibration test chips?

    • Verified Keystroke Sequence: 1000 [ENTER] 82 [g] [÷R]
    • Verified Quotient (\(X\)): 12 complete calculator chassis printed!
    • Verified Remainder (\(Y\)): 16 grams of scrap / test filament remaining (T: 0 | Z: 0 | Y: 16 | X: 12).

    3. Curricular Module 2: Instant Inverse Verification with [LASTX]

    // STANDARDS FOR MATHEMATICAL PRACTICE
    CCSS.MATH.PRACTICE.MP1: Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them.
    CCSS.MATH.PRACTICE.MP3: Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others.
    CCSS.MATH.PRACTICE.MP6: Attend to precision.

    The Misconception: "I Got an Answer, So I'm Done"

    A persistent struggle in elementary math is teaching students to check their work using inverse operations: $\(\text{If } A + B = C \implies C - B = A\)$ $\(\text{If } A \times B = C \implies C \div B = A\)$

    On standard calculators, as soon as the user presses =, the input operands vanish into the ether. Checking the answer requires re-entering the entire problem from scratch, which students find tedious and resist doing.

    The SC-6 Solution: The [LASTX] Key

    The SC-6 maintains a hardware memory register called LASTX. Whenever an arithmetic operation (+, -, ×, ÷, ÷R, MOD) is executed, the calculator automatically copies the value that was in Register \(X\) right before the calculation into LASTX.

    Pressing [f] [LASTX] pushes that saved operand back into Register \(X\), lifting the answer into Register \(Y\)!

    Recipe 1: Self-Checking Multi-Digit Addition

    Student Task: Solve \(487 + 265\) and verify using inverse subtraction (487 [ENTER] 265 [+] [f] [LASTX] [-] → 487).

    PHASE 1: SOLVE THE ADDITION
1. [4] [8] [7] [ENTER]    --> Y: 487 | X: 487
2. [2] [6] [5]            --> Y: 487 | X: 265
3. [+]                    --> X: 752  (Sum)
   [Calculator quietly stores 265 in LASTX]

PHASE 2: VERIFY WITH INVERSE SUBTRACTION (ONE KEYSTROKE!)
4. [f] [LASTX]            --> Y: 752 | X: 265  (Original addend retrieved!)
5. [-]                    --> X: 487  (Original starting number restored!)

    Teacher Facilitation Prompt:
    "Did your screen return to 487? If yes, your addition is mathematically guaranteed to be correct. You proved it yourself without asking the teacher!"

    Recipe 2: The Complete Division Proof (\(Q \times D + R = \text{Dividend}\))

    Student Task: Divide \(79 \div 8\) using [g] [÷R], then prove the result using [f] [LASTX] (79 [ENTER] 8 [g] [÷R] [f] [LASTX] [×] [+] → 79).

    PHASE 1: INTEGER DIVISION
1. [7] [9] [ENTER] [8] [g] [÷R]
   SCREEN READOUT:
   Y: 7  (Remainder R)
   X: 9  (Quotient Q)
   [LASTX automatically holds the Divisor: 8]

PHASE 2: THE 3-KEYSTROKE PROOF
2. [f] [LASTX]            --> Z: 7 (R) | Y: 9 (Q) | X: 8 (Divisor D)
3. [×]                    --> Y: 7 (R) | X: 72 (Q × D)
4. [+]                    --> X: 79  (Original Dividend restored!)

    This 3-keystroke verification sequence ([f] [LASTX] → [×] → [+]) concretizes the Division Algorithm identity: $\(\text{Dividend} = (\text{Quotient} \times \text{Divisor}) + \text{Remainder}\)$ Students actively experience how multiplication and addition undo division and subtraction.

    Recipe 3: Recovering from a Keystroke Slip

    Elementary students frequently press the wrong operator by mistake (e.g., hitting [-] instead of [+]). On traditional calculators, they must press CLEAR and start over. On the SC-6 (150 [ENTER] 25 [-] [f] [LASTX] [+] [f] [LASTX] [+] → 175):

    • Problem: Student wants \(150 + 25\).
    • Mistake: They enter 150 [ENTER] 25 [-] and get 125.
    • Recovery:
    • Press [f] [LASTX] → 25 returns to \(X\), 125 lifts to \(Y\).
    • Press [+] → 150 (Starting base restored!).
    • Press [f] [LASTX] → 25 returns to \(X\), 150 lifts to \(Y\).
    • Press [+] → 175 (Correct sum reached without re-typing 150 or 25!).

    4. Curricular Module 3: Rhythmic Skip-Counting with [REP]

    // STANDARDS ALIGNMENT
    CCSS-M: 2.OA.C.4, 3.OA.A.1, 3.OA.A.7 • Bridges: Grade 2 Unit 1, Grade 3 Unit 2 • Singapore: Primary 2A Ch 4, Primary 3A Ch 3

    Why Kinesthetic Rhythm Matters

    Multiplication is fundamentally equal groups of items. Before children can memorize \(7 \times 6 = 42\), they must feel and hear the cadence of the sequence: $\(7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42\)$

    The SC-6 features a dedicated Replication Key [REP] located at [g] [REP]. Pressing [g] [REP] populates every level of the 4-level stack with the active number: $\(T = K, \quad Z = K, \quad Y = K, \quad X = K\)$

    Because an RPN 4-stack duplicates the \(T\) register when the stack drops during dyadic addition, pressing [+] repeatedly adds the constant indefinitely!

                         HOW [REP] CREATES AN INFINITE FACT LADDER

1. [7] [g] [REP]           2. PRESS [+]              3. PRESS [+] AGAIN
+---------------------+    +---------------------+    +---------------------+
| T:         7        |    | T:         7        |    | T:         7        |
| Z:         7        |    | Z:         7        |    | Z:         7        |
| Y:         7        |    | Y:         7        |    | Y:         7        |
| X:         7        |    | X:        14  (7×2) |    | X:        21  (7×3) |
+---------------------+    +---------------------+    +---------------------+
                                 (T duplicates on drop!)    (T duplicates on drop!)

    Classroom Activity: The Rhythm of Multiples

    1. Teacher Prompt: "Let's build the 6-times multiplication ladder up to 60."
    2. Setup: Student types 6 [g] [REP]. All 4 stack registers are now primed with 6.
    3. Execution: The student chants aloud with each tactile snap:
    4. Press [+] → "6 times 2 is 12!" (Screen: 12)
    5. Press [+] → "6 times 3 is 18!" (Screen: 18)
    6. Press [+] → "6 times 4 is 24!" (Screen: 24)
    7. Press [+] → "6 times 5 is 30!" (Screen: 30)
    8. Press [+] → "6 times 6 is 36!" (Screen: 36)
    9. Press [+] → "6 times 7 is 42!" (Screen: 42)
    10. Press [+] → "6 times 8 is 48!" (Screen: 48)
    11. Press [+] → "6 times 9 is 54!" (Screen: 54)
    12. Press [+] → "6 times 10 is 60!" (Screen: 60)

    The 400-gram snap-dome keys deliver an audible acoustic "click" and physical tactile feedback that anchors the auditory chant to muscle memory.

    Advanced Skip-Counting Explorations

    Exploration A: Backwards Countdown Ladders (Subtracting Constantly)

    Task: Start at 100, skip-count backwards by 8. 1. Enter starting point: 100 [ENTER]. 2. Prime the subtrahend: 8 [g] [REP]. 3. Press [-] repeatedly: * Press [-] → 92 * Press [-] → 84 * Press [-] → 76 * Press [-] → 68 ... down to 4 (the remainder of \(100 \div 8\)!).

    Exploration B: Geometric Exponential Doubling (Powers of 2)

    Task: Discover exponential growth (doubling lily pads on a pond). 1. Prime the base: 2 [g] [REP]. 2. Press [×] repeatedly: * Press [×] → 4 (\(2^2\)) * Press [×] → 8 (\(2^3\)) * Press [×] → 16 (\(2^4\)) * Press [×] → 32 (\(2^5\)) * Press [×] → 64 (\(2^6\)) * Press [×] → 128 (\(2^7\)) ... up to 1024 (\(2^{10}\))!

    5. Curricular Module 4: Order of Operations without Parentheses

    // STANDARDS ALIGNMENT
    CCSS-M: 5.OA.A.1, 6.EE.A.2 • Big Ideas Math: Grade 5 Ch 1, Grade 6 Ch 3

    Multi-Term Word Problem Example

    Scenario: A school STEM club buys: * 3 microcontrollers at $4.50 each * 2 battery holders at $1.25 each * 4 LED packs at $0.75 each

    What is the total cost of the order? $\(\text{Total} = (3 \times 4.50) + (2 \times 1.25) + (4 \times 0.75)\)$

    How Infix vs. SC-6 4-Stack Handles the Problem (3 [ENTER] 4.5 [×] 2 [ENTER] 1.25 [×] [+] 4 [ENTER] 0.75 [×] [+] → 19)

    Step Traditional Infix Calculator SC-6 4-Stack XYZT (RPN) Stack Registers (\(T, Z, Y, X\))
    1 Press ( 3 × 4.50 ) + (Must remember parentheses!) 3 [ENTER] 4.5 [×] \(X: 13.50\)
    2 Press ( 2 × 1.25 ) + (Hope parentheses don't nest wrong!) 2 [ENTER] 1.25 [×] \(Y: 13.50, \quad X: 2.50\)
    3 Press ( 4 × 0.75 ) = [+] \(X: 16.00\) (Running total!)
    4 Hope order-of-operations evaluated correctly 4 [ENTER] 0.75 [×] \(Y: 16.00, \quad X: 3.00\)
    5 — [+] \(X: 19\) (Final total verified!)

    The Pedagogical Difference: On the SC-6, students never type a parenthesis. Every sub-calculation is evaluated immediately, and the intermediate products wait patiently on the stack. The child can pause at Step 2 and see both $13.50 and $2.50 resting on the screen.

    6. Quick Reference: SC-6 Elementary Keystroke Recipes

    Print this reference card for classroom math station desks:

    | Mathematical Goal | Exact SC-6 Keystrokes | Screen Readout & Register State | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **Integer Division with Remainder** | `Dividend [ENTER] Divisor [g] [÷R]` | **$X$:** Whole Quotient ($Q$) • **$Y$:** Remainder ($R$) | | **Swap Quotient & Remainder** | `[x⇄y]` | Reverses $X$ and $Y$ to bring remainder into active position | | **Retrieve Divisor / Previous Value** | `[f] [LASTX]` | Restores previous operand into $X$; lifts answer into $Y$ | | **Verify Addition with Inverse** | `A [ENTER] B [+] [f] [LASTX] [-]` | Returns $A$ to prove $C - B = A$ | | **Verify Division with Formula** | `[f] [LASTX] [×] [+]` | Computes $(Q \times D) + R$ to restore original Dividend | | **Prime Fact Ladder / Skip-Count** | `Step [g] [REP]` | Fills $T, Z, Y, X$ with Step value for repeated addition | | **Step Through Multiples** | Press `[+]` repeatedly | $2\times, 3\times, 4\times, 5\times \dots$ with acoustic snap clicks | | **Powers of 2 (Doubling)** | `2 [g] [REP] [×] [×] [×] ...` | Generates $4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024$ | | **Simplify Fraction Step-by-Step** | `[.] N [.] D [g] [SIMP]` | Reduces fraction by smallest common factor; flashes factor | | **Toggle Stack Depth (4-Stack / 2-Stack)** | `[g] [FLAGS]` • `[5]` (`DEP`) • `[C]` | Toggles between default 4-stack XYZT (`4-LV`) and simplified 2-stack (`2-LV`) for early learners |

    7. Station Challenge Cards (Reproducible Classroom Prompts)

    Challenge Card 1: The Cookie Bakery Box Packing (Grades 3–4)

    Prompt: A baker bakes 58 chocolate chip cookies. Each gift box holds 6 cookies.
    1. Use [g] [÷R] on your SC-6 to find how many full gift boxes she can pack.
    2. How many cookies are left over for the baker to eat?
    3. Press [f] [LASTX] [×] [+] to prove your answer matches 58!
    Expected Keystrokes: 58 [ENTER] 6 [g] [÷R] ==> X: 9 boxes, Y: 4 leftover cookies.
    Proof: 58 [ENTER] 6 [g] [÷R] [f] [LASTX] [×] [+] ==> 58 cookies.

    Challenge Card 2: The Multiplication Table 7s Race (Grades 2–3)

    Prompt: Load 7 into your SC-6 using [g] [REP].
    1. Press [+] four times. What multiple of 7 is on the screen? (\(7 \times 5 = 35\))
    2. Press [+] three more times. What is \(7 \times 8\)? (\(56\))
    3. Can you reach 70 without making an error?
    Expected Keystrokes: 7 [g] [REP] [+] [+] [+] [+] ==> 7. [+] [+] [+] ==> 56.

    Challenge Card 3: The Detective Proof Station (Grades 4–5)

    Prompt: A student calculated \(345 + 189 = 534\).
    1. Enter 345 [ENTER] 189 [+] on your SC-6.
    2. Use [f] [LASTX] to recall the second addend.
    3. Press [-] to perform the inverse operation (345 [ENTER] 189 [+] [f] [LASTX] [-]). Did you get 345?
    4. Write an explanation: Why does LASTX guarantee the answer is correct?

    Summary: Empowering Young Mathematicians

    The transition to a default 4-stack XYZT display in the SC-6 transforms the calculator from a passive answer generator into an active, transparent laboratory instrument. With [÷R], [LASTX], and [REP], young students explore the fundamental structures of arithmetic—quotients, remainders, inverses, and multiples—with tactile certainty and joy.
